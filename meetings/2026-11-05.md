## Decisions

1. **Blocks:** Kira and Parth pick their blocks first; Harsh takes the block left over; whoever finishes first also takes the model index spreadsheet. Claim on GitHub (`python3 scripts/blocks.py claim <block>`, or comment on issue #28) by Fri Nov 6 noon.
2. **Contributor type:** everyone is an individual contributor. We work asynchronously and discuss in the company Google Chat.
3. **Split (Plan A):** each person completes only their block. If the professor says everyone must answer all 21 tasks, we share analysis and code and each person writes their own answers for the rest.
4. **Product shape:** an LTI 1.3 tool inside the LMS.
5. **Human in the loop:** an instructor reviews every flag and approves every message to a student; the tool never writes a grade.
6. **AI outage at a deadline:** the LMS accepts and time-stamps the submission, the check is queued, and the student is told nothing until a person has looked.
7. **Latency:** design for 3 s per document, which also meets the 5 s case target, until the professor answers.
8. **Hosting:** an open-weights model on university-contracted servers (FERPA, under $0.02 per submission), compared against one commercial API in D2. Use models from the professor's approved list (Canvas announcement, Nov 6).
9. **Model names:** the D-block lead picks the generation and embedding models in D1 and posts them in the chat; every module uses the same names.
10. **Retention:** essays and embeddings are kept until the end of the term plus the appeal window, then deleted; the university contract sets the exact period.
11. **Communication:** updates go on each GitHub issue and in the company Google Chat (graded); WhatsApp is for quick chat only.
